Hackers Take $6.3 Billion From Binance

North Korea blamed as the exchange halts withdrawals and about half the stolen crypto is frozen

Binance said about $6.3 billion in cryptocurrency was taken from the world’s largest exchange, the biggest theft the industry has recorded, and suspended all deposits and withdrawals on Thursday while it traced the funds. The blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis said they were highly confident that the North Korean hacking unit TraderTraitor, which investigators link to the Lazarus Group, carried out the attack, using a method that mirrored the 2025 theft at Bybit that the FBI attributed to Pyongyang; North Korea routinely denies such claims and its missions in Geneva and China did not answer questions from Reuters. An initial investigation pointed to a Binance engineer’s compromised laptop as the entry point, and Reuters reported that the bulk of the transfers took about 20 minutes beginning late Thursday. Richard Teng, a co-chief executive, wrote on X that user money was safe and that the company would cover every loss; the stablecoin issuers Tether, Circle and Ripple froze roughly $2.57 billion between them, and Binance said it had located about $3 billion of the total and expected withdrawals to resume within 48 hours. Bitcoin fell to a six-week low of $76,368 and ether slipped more than 3 percent to $2,178, while shares in Coinbase, which said it was unaffected, closed down 4.8 percent. Ari Redbord, TRM’s global head of policy, called the attack unprecedented; the previous record was the $1.5 billion taken from Bybit last year.

Train Crash in Spain Kills 64

Renfe express strikes an Iryo service halted by a signal fault south of Madrid; 50 gravely hurt

A Renfe AVE express running from Madrid to Seville struck the rear of a stationary Iryo train shortly after 9 a.m. on Wednesday near Urda, in Toledo province south of Madrid, and at least 64 people were killed, Reuters reported at midday; the Spanish broadcaster Telecinco put the gravely injured at 50. The Iryo service, carrying 147 people, had been stopped on the orders of the infrastructure manager ADIF because of a signal fault, and Renfe said its own train, carrying 397, had been authorized to continue before the impact, adding that the sequence of events would have to be established by the investigation. Cars from both trains left the track and several went down an embankment. The toll climbed through the morning as rescuers worked through the wreckage: Esther Padilla, a spokeswoman for the Castilla-La Mancha regional government, gave 10 dead in the first hours and officials reported about 30 by mid-morning, figures that mark successive points in the search rather than a dispute between sources. ADIF suspended high-speed traffic between Madrid and Andalusia, and Health Minister Mónica García said crews were concentrating on the two most damaged cars. It is the second fatal rail disaster in Spain this year, after an Iryo derailment near Adamuz in January that killed 45.
