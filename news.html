Europe Sinks Climate Deal Over Fossil Fuels

First COP failure since Copenhagen as Kurum concedes no consensus after two days of overtime

COP31 ended in Antalya, Türkiye on Sunday without adopting a final declaration, the first United Nations climate summit to break down that way since Copenhagen in 2009. Murat Kurum, the summit president and Türkiye’s environment minister, told the closing plenary that “there is no consensus on this document,” said no further text would be put forward, and left the stage while country representatives were still speaking. The draft, tabled by the co-presidencies of Türkiye and Australia, made no explicit reference to fossil fuels or to transitioning away from them, acknowledged that warming will exceed the 1.5 degree Celsius threshold agreed at Paris, and called for faster development of carbon removal technologies; Darragh O’Brien, Ireland’s climate minister, said the European Union could not support a text with no reference to fossil fuels, and Panama said it failed to meet the scale and urgency of the crisis. Observers at the venue said Saudi Arabia and other large oil producers had pushed to keep fossil fuels out of the text while keeping carbon removal in, and a Saudi delegate said it made no sense for concrete solutions to be taken off the table. Reuters reported that the collapse of the political declaration does not erase obligations agreed at previous summits; the talks, involving 196 countries, ran two days past Friday’s deadline, and no host for the next summit was named in the closing reports.
