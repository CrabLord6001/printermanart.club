Critical PHP Flaw Drives Governments Offline

An OpenAI model found the bug first; the Netherlands, Finland, France and Australia pull services to patch

Accounts of exploitation differ in scope rather than substance: Cloudflare’s chief executive said the company saw thousands of attempts to use the flaw within hours, while the security firm Patchstack said its own telemetry showed probing without hard evidence of a breach.

Tillis calls it beyond irresponsible and Cornyn invokes Graham, but both chambers stay in recess until after the election

WashingtonThe executive order President Trump signed late Friday, setting aside indefinitely the sanctions in the Lindsey Graham Russian Sanctions Act days before they were to take effect, drew objections across both parties on Saturday. The law, which would have imposed steep tariffs on countries buying large volumes of Russian oil and gas, passed the Senate 86 to 11 and the House with bipartisan support, and Trump signed it about a month ago; the waiver follows his agreement to import Russian diesel, which he has presented as a way to lower pump prices before 3 November. Senator Thom Tillis of North Carolina called the move beyond irresponsible and said a law carried 86 to 11 ought to be enforced, Senator John Cornyn of Texas said the late Senator Graham would be appalled, and Senator Rick Scott of Florida said it was not the step he would have taken; Senator Elizabeth Warren and Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer accused the president of defying Congress and giving the Russian economy life support. Representative Gregory Meeks, the ranking Democrat on House Foreign Affairs, introduced a bill on Friday to block the diesel arrangement and said Congress is not helpless, but with both chambers in recess until after election day no vote is scheduled.

Pope Leo Resumes Duties After Lung Surgery

Discharged after four days, he thanks crowds in St Peter’s Square as biopsy results decide any further treatment

Vatican CityPope Leo XIV appeared at the window of the Apostolic Palace for the Angelus on Sunday, thanked thousands in St Peter’s Square for their prayers and said he would gradually resume his activities. He was discharged from the Gemelli hospital on Saturday after four days, a shorter stay than expected, and told a reporter he was doing much better; surgeons removed the lower lobe of his right lung on Tuesday and confirmed adenocarcinoma, the most common form of lung cancer, in the first serious illness of his seventeen-month pontificate. His surgeon, Dr Stefano Margaritora, expects a full recovery in about ten days and told Vatican News there is no clinical reason his 6 to 17 November journey to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru should not go ahead. Trump Waives Sanctions on Russia

Executive order shelves the Graham Act tariffs before they bite; Zelensky says Washington is rewarding Putin

President Trump signed an executive order on Friday waiving almost all of the penalties in the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, the measure Congress passed this summer, before it took effect. The order nominally raised duties on Russian goods by 500 per cent, and by 100 per cent on goods from the five largest buyers of Russian crude and natural gas, then set those measures aside; the act gives the president broad latitude to impose or waive them. Senator Richard Blumenthal, a co-author of the law, and Representative Don Beyer said the waiver defies the statute’s intent and amounts to capitulation, while Kirill Dmitriev, Moscow’s economic negotiator, called it a historic moment and said economic talks with Washington would go on. The waiver follows Trump’s announcement on 9 October that Russia would supply millions of tonnes of diesel to the United States and world markets, an arrangement twelve senators asked him to rescind on 13 October; President Volodymyr Zelensky, who said this week he was finished with the energy ceasefire Washington announced, said Trump was rewarding Putin.

WashingtonActing Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter wrote to the Los Angeles County counsel’s office warning that processing or tabulating ballots cast by suspected noncitizens could expose local election officials to criminal investigation and prosecution, saying that knowingly facilitating such conduct is equally unlawful. The letter, first obtained by the Associated Press, was copied to Secretary of State Shirley Weber and county registrar Dean Logan, and arrived less than three weeks before the 3 November election; any case would fall to Bill Essayli, the senior federal prosecutor for the Los Angeles area. Rick Hasen, a law professor at UCLA, criticised the letter.
