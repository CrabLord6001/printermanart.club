Bolsonaro Flees Brazil for United States

State Department will issue visas to the defeated candidate and his family as a passport order goes unanswered

WashingtonA senior State Department official said on Tuesday that the department will issue visas to Flávio Bolsonaro and his family and allow them to enter the United States, speaking anonymously because of visa privacy law, as Brazilian officials said the senator had left the country days after losing the presidential election to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. A sealed Supreme Court order issued Sunday and served on Bolsonaro the following day required him to surrender his regular and diplomatic passports by noon Tuesday; he said he would comply while calling the order an attempt to intimidate him, and whether he did so is unclear. He is under Supreme Court investigation in two cases involving alleged corruption, money laundering and tax evasion, and denies wrongdoing. Lula won by about 4.4 million votes, a margin of nearly 4 percent, and Bolsonaro has made unsubstantiated fraud claims and asked the electoral court to annul the result, which is due to be certified on December 18. His father, former president Jair Bolsonaro, is serving a 27-year sentence under house arrest for an attempted coup, and his brother Eduardo, who lives in Texas, was convicted in absentia in June of illegally lobbying the Trump administration.

Israelis Vote in Record Numbers

Turnout reaches 31.5 percent by noon, the highest at that hour since 1973, with polls closing at 10 p.m.

Israelis went to the polls on Tuesday in the first national election since the Hamas attack of October 7, 2023, and nearly 2.4 million had voted by midday — 31.5 percent of the electorate, against 28.4 percent at the same hour in 2022 and the highest share at that point in the day since 1973, according to the Central Elections Committee. About 7.3 million people are eligible, among them 1.2 million Palestinian citizens of Israel; Israelis abroad cannot vote. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who voted in Jerusalem with his wife, Sara, around noon and called the day a pivotal moment for the country, is seeking to hold power against a broad bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief of staff. An average of Channel 11, 12 and 13 polls reported by Haaretz on Friday gave Netanyahu’s partners 50 of the Knesset’s 120 seats and Eisenkot’s side 54, with 61 needed for a majority, and Arab-led parties could decide the outcome if neither bloc reaches it. Polls close at 10 p.m. local time, exit polls follow within hours, the final tally is expected by Friday and official results are published on November 4.

Judge Rules Comey Firing Unlawful

Jesse Furman reinstates the former Epstein prosecutor with back pay and rejects an unlimited removal power

New YorkUS District Judge Jesse Furman held on Monday, in a 55-page decision, that the Justice Department acted unlawfully in firing Maurene Comey from her post as a federal prosecutor in the Southern District of New York in July 2025, finding that the removal violated the separation of powers and the Civil Service Reform Act of 1978. Furman rejected the government’s argument that Article II gives the president unlimited power to remove such officers, calling it a sweeping and unprecedented theory of executive authority, and retroactively reinstated Comey with back pay covering the period from the firing until December 20, when she took another job. The department had said Attorney General Pam Bondi directed the removal and that her successor, Todd Blanche, ratified it, while acknowledging that Comey, a daughter of former FBI director James Comey, received outstanding performance ratings from 2019 through 2024. Her lawyers called the ruling a hard-earned victory for civil servants; a department spokesperson said it disagrees with the decision and will appeal.

Truce Takes Hold Around Occupied Oleshky

Russia halts Kherson strikes through Saturday to let aid reach as many as 2,000 trapped civilians

KyivA localized ceasefire took effect at 01:00 GMT on Tuesday around Oleshky, a Russian-held town on the eastern bank of the Dnipro River opposite Kherson city, and Russia’s Defense Ministry said it had suspended strikes on Kherson province for its duration. The pause is to run through Saturday or longer and is meant to let doctors, medicine, food and water reach the town before evacuation corridors open; the United Nations says up to 2,000 people remain there and cannot leave because of mines and drone attacks. Oleshky had about 24,000 residents before the war, was seized in the first days of the February 2022 invasion and was badly flooded after the Kakhovka Dam was destroyed in 2023. The agreement was announced on October 23 and brokered by First Lady Melania Trump with the involvement of Pope Leo, as Washington presses to revive talks after more than four and a half years of fighting; strikes continued elsewhere in Ukraine, and earlier attempts at a wider truce have foundered on mistrust between Moscow and Kyiv.

Ninth Circuit Turns Back Ballot Appeal

Panel calls the emergency self-constructed and sends the fight over 20,000 California mail ballots back down

San FranciscoA three-judge panel of the Ninth US Circuit Court of Appeals denied the Justice Department’s emergency appeal on Monday in its suit to stop California from counting more than 20,000 mail ballots the federal government says are tied to people it believes are likely noncitizens. Judges Mark Bennett, a Trump appointee, and Gabriel Sanchez, appointed by President Biden, held that the appeals court had no authority to act before the district court had ruled, and said the claimed emergency was at least in part self-constructed, pointing to the department’s own timing in filing suit on October 15, its late recognition that California law governs ballots and its failure to tell the court what relief it sought; Judge Patrick Bumatay, a Trump appointee, dissented. At a weekend argument held by video, Bennett had observed that the Elections Clause leaves the running of elections to the states. The case returns to federal district court in California, where a hearing was set for Tuesday afternoon, a week before the November 3 midterm elections; Governor Gavin Newsom has called the suit a desperate attempt to suppress voters.

MilwaukeeArchbishop Jeffrey Grob of Milwaukee issued a statement titled “A Public Apology” on Sunday, saying the White House had publicized a meeting he and 11 other bishops attended on October 22 without the participants’ consent and less than two weeks before the midterm elections.
