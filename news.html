World Court Finds Myanmar Committed Genocide

Nine-to-six ruling orders Rohingya returns and prosecutions in the first such finding against a state

The HagueThe International Court of Justice ruled on Thursday, by nine votes to six, that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in northern Rakhine State in breach of the 1948 Genocide Convention — the first time in the court’s eighty-year history that judges have found a state responsible for genocide. Court President Yuji Iwasawa read the verdict, which rejected Myanmar’s argument that its 2017 campaign was a legitimate operation against insurgents and found genocidal intent in specific instances; the court said security forces killed thousands of civilians, cited evidence that about 10,000 Rohingya were killed, and described villages as systematically destroyed. Myanmar was ordered to allow and facilitate the safe, voluntary and dignified return of those who fled and to investigate and punish anyone suspected of genocide; The Gambia brought the case in 2019, there is no appeal, and the court can do no more than report noncompliance to the United Nations Security Council. More than 700,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh during the 2017 crackdown, and about 1.3 million now live in 33 camps at Cox’s Bazar, the largest refugee settlement in the world.

States Get Six Months On Medicaid Work Rules

Requirement still begins January 1, but coverage removals may wait until July as directors cite implementation problems

WashingtonThe Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services told state Medicaid directors Friday that they may wait until July 1 before removing people from coverage, or denying applications, for failing to meet the program’s new work requirement, which still takes effect nationally on January 1. States must continue to determine whether enrollees and applicants are working, in school or volunteering; the agency said states are making progress but face implementation challenges. The rule applies to adults covered under the voluntary expansion adopted by 40 states and requires at least 80 hours of work or volunteering a month, or half-time enrollment in school, with exemptions for people over 64, those caring for children under 14 and those deemed too medically frail to work. Nebraska, the first state to begin, found that more than nine in ten of those due for renewal in July and August qualified, while 533 were denied continuing coverage, mostly over paperwork and 43 for not meeting the requirement.

Communications Failure Grounds Every American Flight

Longest stop in twenty-five years cancels more than 4,700 flights as Duffy blames overloaded switches and routers

A telecommunications failure inside the Federal Aviation Administration’s legacy network forced the agency to halt all departures nationwide at 3:54 p.m. Eastern time Friday, the longest ground stop in twenty-five years and only the second order covering all departures since September 11, 2001. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said switches and routers in the aging system had been overwhelmed by data, leaving intermittent radar gaps and patchy communication with aircraft rather than a total loss of either; airspace reopened at 8:15 p.m. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no cyberattack and no indication of a malicious act, and that about 3,000 aircraft already aloft landed safely. More than 4,700 flights were canceled and some 10,000 delayed Friday, with roughly 700 more canceled for Saturday; Aviation A2Z reported that Duffy used a Friday briefing to raise the administration’s air traffic control modernization request to $50 billion, up from $30.1 billion.

Measles Costs America Its Elimination Status

Regional health body acts after a year of continuous transmission; federal and state death counts diverge

WashingtonThe Pan American Health Organization has revoked the United States’ measles elimination status, ending a designation held for twenty-six years, according to The Wall Street Journal, which cited people familiar with the decision; Reuters reported that the organization did not comment. The determination rested on a confidential Centers for Disease Control and Prevention finding that the same strain had circulated continuously for twelve months, and Noel Brewer, the University of North Carolina professor who chairs the independent panel that presented the outbreak data, attributed the spread to low vaccination coverage. The CDC had confirmed 5,182 cases in 2026 as of Thursday across 45 states, the District of Columbia and New York City, against 2,289 for all of 2025; kindergarten coverage for the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine has fallen to 92.4 percent from 95.2 percent in the 2019-2020 school year. The counts of the dead differ: Reuters reported the CDC has confirmed six measles-related deaths this year, while Al Jazeera, citing state health departments, put measles-associated deaths at ten, and the agency says it is working with state epidemiologists on a standard definition.

Guterres Warns Of Inevitable Warming Overshoot

UN chief tells the Antalya summit that current pledges point to about 2.5 degrees Celsius by century’s end

Antalya, TurkeyUnited Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said a temporary breach of the 1.5 degree Celsius warming threshold is now unavoidable and called for immediate and drastic cuts to greenhouse gas emissions, speaking at a news conference on the sidelines of the COP31 climate summit. He said the aim must be to keep the overshoot as small and as brief as possible, that national commitments as they stand put the world on course for roughly 2.5 degrees of warming by 2100, and that global emissions would need to fall by about 6 percent a year over the next few years. Guterres urged developed countries to honor their climate finance pledges and emerging economies, China among them, to accelerate the shift from fossil fuels to renewables, saying expansion of renewable energy has to be paired with reduced fossil fuel production and that there is no credible route to a safe climate without it.
