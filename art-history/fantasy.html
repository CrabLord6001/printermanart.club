The Books of Earthsea: The Complete Illustrated Edition

| Charles Vess
iCatalogue details

American, born 1951 · 2018 · More than fifty color and black-and-white illustrations
Saga Press, New York · In copyright · greenmanpress.com

Four years of work, made in close collaboration with Ursula K. Le Guin, who chose the scenes herself and died months before the book appeared. The dragon alone took about a year of revision, each sketch pulling it further from a reptile and closer to a face. Vess keeps the figures small inside large landscapes, so it is the islands rather than the wizards that carry the pictures.

Oedipus and the Sphinx

| Gustave Moreau
iCatalogue details

French, 1826–1898 · 1864 · Oil on canvas, 206.4 × 104.8 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 21.134.1 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

Moreau made his name with this picture at the Paris Salon of 1864, at a moment when French painting was turning toward scenes of ordinary modern life. He went the other way, into myth, and a younger generation that included Odilon Redon and Oscar Wilde followed him there. The sphinx has jumped and is holding on by her claws: the riddle is being put at close range, and what is left of the travelers who answered wrong lies on the ledge below.

The Beacons of Gondor

| Donato Giancola
iCatalogue details

American, born 1967 · 2022 · Oil on linen, 77 × 114 in.
Artist's own collection · In copyright · donatoarts.com

Nearly ten feet across, painted in traditional oils from posed models rather than digitally. The beacon fire is the only warm light in the picture, so the whole composition is built out of how far that light carries before it gives out.

Shown 19 October 2026

Island of the Dead

| Arnold Böcklin
iCatalogue details

Swiss, 1827–1901 · 1880 · Oil on wood, 73.7 × 121.9 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 26.90 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

The island exists nowhere. A young widow commissioned the picture as a memorial to her husband, and the draped coffin and the white standing figure were added at her request. Cliffs and cypresses drop to near-silhouette, so the only truly lit thing in the painting is the small boat. This is the first of five versions Böcklin painted; the one usually reproduced online is the 1883 Berlin picture.

Shown 19 October 2026
