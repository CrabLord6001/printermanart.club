Young Woman with a Water Pitcher

| Johannes Vermeer
iCatalog details

Dutch, Delft 1632–1675 Delft · ca. 1662 · Oil on canvas, 45.7 × 40.6 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 89.15.21 · Public domain (CC0) · metmuseum.org

She is doing two things at once and neither is finished: opening the window with her left hand, lifting the pitcher with her right. Vermeer builds the whole picture out of that pause. Light comes through the leaded glass and lands blue on the white linen, and the map on the back wall is the kind Dutch households actually hung. The first Vermeer to enter an American public collection, in 1889.

The Musicians

| Caravaggio (Michelangelo Merisi)
iCatalog details

Italian, Milan or Caravaggio 1571–1610 Porto Ercole · 1597 · Oil on canvas, 92.1 x 118.4 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 52.81 · Public domain (CC0) · metmuseum.org

One of the first pictures Caravaggio painted for Cardinal Del Monte, who took him into his household and effectively started his career. The boy with the cornetto, looking out of the picture at us, is usually taken to be Caravaggio himself. The canvas has had a hard life: it was badly damaged and heavily restored long before the Museum got it, which is why some passages read softer than the rest.

Mona Lisa

| Leonardo da Vinci
iCatalog details

Italian, Vinci 1452–1519 Amboise · ca. 1503–19 · Oil on poplar panel, 77 × 53 cm
Musée du Louvre, INV. 779 · Public domain · collections.louvre.fr

Leonardo kept the panel for the rest of his life and took it with him to France, which is why it is in Paris rather than Florence. The landscape behind her is doing quiet work: the horizon on the left sits lower than the one on the right, so the eye keeps resettling and her expression seems to change with it. The surface carries almost no visible brushwork, built instead from glazes laid so thinly that the transitions have no edges at all.

Juan de Pareja

| Velázquez (Diego Rodríguez de Silva y Velázquez)
iCatalog details

Spanish, Seville 1599–1660 Madrid · 1650 · Oil on canvas, 81.3 × 69.9 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1971.86 · Public domain (CC0) · metmuseum.org

Velázquez painted this in Rome, partly as a warm-up before he began the portrait of Pope Innocent X, and showed it publicly at the Pantheon that March to considerable noise. The sitter was his own assistant, enslaved in his household; within months Velázquez signed the papers that freed him, and Pareja went on working as a painter in his own right. Almost everything in the picture is a gray-brown, so the white collar and the one lit edge of the cheek are left carrying all of it.

Edward VI (1537–1553), When Duke of Cornwall

| Hans Holbein the Younger
iCatalog details

German, Augsburg 1497/98–1543 London · ca. 1545; reworked 1547 or later · Oil and gold on oak, 32.4 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 49.7.31 · Public domain (CC0) · metmuseum.org

A roundel not quite thirteen inches across, painted in oil and gold on an oak panel. The boy was Duke of Cornwall when the picture was begun and king before anyone was finished with it — the panel was worked on again in or after 1547, the year Edward came to the throne at nine. He was dead at fifteen. The blue behind him has no depth in it at all, which is what throws the face forward.

Aristotle with a Bust of Homer

| Rembrandt (Rembrandt van Rijn)
iCatalog details

Dutch, Leiden 1606–1669 Amsterdam · 1653 · Oil on canvas, 143.5 × 136.5 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 61.198 · Public domain (CC0) · metmuseum.org

Painted for a Sicilian collector who wanted a philosopher and left the subject to Rembrandt. Aristotle rests a hand on Homer, who was poor and blind, while the gold chain over his own shoulder is the kind a ruler gives a courtier — the picture sets what a thinker is paid against what a poet was worth. Almost everything is black or brown except the white sleeve and the bust, and the light falls only where Rembrandt wants the question asked.

Shown 25 October 2026

Blind Orion Searching for the Rising Sun

| Nicolas Poussin
iCatalog details

French, 1594–1665 · 1658 · Oil on canvas, 119.1 × 182.9 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 24.45.1 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

Orion, blinded, walking east toward the sunrise that is to give him back his sight, with Cedalion riding his shoulders to steer him and Hephaestus watching from below. Diana stands on the storm cloud that hangs across his eyes; one long-standing reading takes her presence as notice of the death she will later deal him. Poussin painted it in Rome in 1658 for Michel Passart, a Paris finance official who owned at least seven of his pictures.

The Harvesters

| Pieter Bruegel the Elder
iCatalog details

Netherlandish, ca. 1525–1569 · 1565 · Oil on oak, 119 × 162 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 19.164 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

One of six panels Bruegel painted for a wealthy Antwerp merchant, marking the turns of the year; five survive. It is among the earliest European paintings to treat ordinary labor as a subject worth the whole canvas rather than a detail at the edge of a religious scene. The wheat is waist-high and the cut path through it leads the eye straight back to the village.
