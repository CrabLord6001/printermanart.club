Young Woman with a Water Pitcher

| Johannes Vermeer
iCatalogue details

Dutch, Delft 1632–1675 Delft · ca. 1662 · Oil on canvas, 45.7 × 40.6 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 89.15.21 · Public domain (CC0) · metmuseum.org

She is doing two things at once and neither is finished: opening the window with her left hand, lifting the pitcher with her right. Vermeer builds the whole picture out of that pause. Light comes through the leaded glass and lands blue on the white linen, and the map on the back wall is the kind Dutch households actually hung. The first Vermeer to enter an American public collection, in 1889.

Aristotle with a Bust of Homer

| Rembrandt (Rembrandt van Rijn)
iCatalogue details

Dutch, Leiden 1606–1669 Amsterdam · 1653 · Oil on canvas, 143.5 × 136.5 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 61.198 · Public domain (CC0) · metmuseum.org

Painted for a Sicilian collector who wanted a philosopher and left the subject to Rembrandt. Aristotle rests a hand on Homer, who was poor and blind, while the gold chain over his own shoulder is the kind a ruler gives a courtier — the picture sets what a thinker is paid against what a poet was worth. Almost everything is black or brown except the white sleeve and the bust, and the light falls only where Rembrandt wants the question asked.

Shown 25 October 2026

Blind Orion Searching for the Rising Sun

| Nicolas Poussin
iCatalogue details

French, 1594–1665 · 1658 · Oil on canvas, 119.1 × 182.9 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 24.45.1 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

Orion, blinded, walking east toward the sunrise that is to give him back his sight, with Cedalion riding his shoulders to steer him and Hephaestus watching from below. Diana stands on the storm cloud that hangs across his eyes; one long-standing reading takes her presence as notice of the death she will later deal him. Poussin painted it in Rome in 1658 for Michel Passart, a Paris finance official who owned at least seven of his pictures.

Shown 23 October 2026

The Harvesters

| Pieter Bruegel the Elder
iCatalogue details

Netherlandish, ca. 1525–1569 · 1565 · Oil on oak, 119 × 162 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 19.164 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

One of six panels Bruegel painted for a wealthy Antwerp merchant, marking the turns of the year; five survive. It is among the earliest European paintings to treat ordinary labor as a subject worth the whole canvas rather than a detail at the edge of a religious scene. The wheat is waist-high and the cut path through it leads the eye straight back to the village.
