Church / Faith Art

The Crucifixion of Saint Andrew

Caravaggio

Italian, 1571–1610 · 1606–7 · Oil on canvas, 202.5 × 152.7 cm
The Cleveland Museum of Art, 1976.2 · Public domain (CC0) · clevelandart.org

Painted in Naples, in the months after Caravaggio fled Rome under sentence of death for killing a man. The legend holds that Andrew preached from the cross for two days until the crowd demanded his release, and that the executioner sent to cut him down found his hands would not move. That is the instant here: the man on the ladder, arms up, stopped.

Fantasy Art

Island of the Dead

Arnold Böcklin

Swiss, 1827–1901 · 1880 · Oil on wood, 73.7 × 121.9 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 26.90 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

The island exists nowhere. A young widow commissioned the picture as a memorial to her husband, and the draped coffin and the white standing figure were added at her request. Cliffs and cypresses drop to near-silhouette, so the only truly lit thing in the painting is the small boat. This is the first of five versions Böcklin painted; the one usually reproduced online is the 1883 Berlin picture.

The Beacons of Gondor

Donato Giancola

American, born 1967 · 2022 · Oil on linen, 77 × 114 in.
Artist’s own collection · In copyright · donatoarts.com

Nearly ten feet across, painted in traditional oils from posed models rather than digitally. The beacon fire is the only warm light in the picture, so the whole composition is built out of how far that light carries before it gives out.

Classics

The Harvesters

Pieter Bruegel the Elder

Netherlandish, ca. 1525–1569 · 1565 · Oil on oak, 119 × 162 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 19.164 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

One of six panels Bruegel painted for a wealthy Antwerp merchant, marking the turns of the year; five survive. It is among the earliest European paintings to treat ordinary labour as a subject worth the whole canvas rather than a detail at the edge of a religious scene. The wheat is waist-high and the cut path through it leads the eye straight back to the village.

Japanese Art

Hen and Rooster with Grapevine

Itō Jakuchū

Japanese, 1716–1800 · 1792 · Edo period · Hanging scroll; ink and color on silk, image 101.9 × 41.3 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1975.268.69 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

Jakuchū ran his family’s Kyoto greengrocery for forty years before handing it over to paint full time, and he was seventy-six when he made this. The bird is assembled from hundreds of separate feather strokes rather than massed shapes, and its tail is drawn crossing in front of the grapevine instead of disappearing behind it.

Landscape + Nature

Twilight in the Wilderness

Frederic Edwin Church

American, 1826–1900 · 1860 · Oil on canvas, 101.6 × 162.6 cm
The Cleveland Museum of Art, 1965.233 · Public domain (CC0) · clevelandart.org

Painted the year before the Civil War began, from studies Church made in Maine, and emptied of every human figure — no cabin, no boat, no road. Almost half the canvas is sky. The red is laid in thin glazes over a pale ground so the clouds appear lit from behind rather than coloured on the surface.

Female Figures

Madame X (Virginie Amélie Avegno Gautreau)

John Singer Sargent

American, 1856–1925 · 1883–84 · Oil on canvas, 208.6 × 109.9 cm
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 16.53 · Public domain · metmuseum.org

The 1884 Paris Salon received it so badly that Sargent left for London and stayed. Gautreau powdered her skin with lavender, and Sargent matched it exactly, which is why she reads almost grey against the black dress and brown ground. The right strap originally hung off her shoulder; he repainted it upright after the Salon, and the picture stayed in his studio for thirty years.
